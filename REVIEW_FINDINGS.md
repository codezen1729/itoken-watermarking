The saved results are internally consistent, but they do not demonstrate reliable recovery or robust watermarking of general LLM-generated ideas. The real-model scoring calculation works on the specified finite candidate support; semantic recovery fails on this held-out pilot.

The checks recomputed sequence and grouped probabilities from saved token log probabilities, recovery metrics from canonical steps and gold, summary counts, and frozen source/dataset/model metadata hashes. The false acceptance in [test22](research_results/qwen_05b_ids_v1/recovery/test22.json) exposes a semantic gap. The source asserts an unsound transition from `p0` to `e0` and then from `e0` alone to `p1`, explicitly saying no connecting implication is supplied. The model selects `I005`, a valid formal MP step from `(e0|p0)` and `((e0|p0)->p1)` to `p1`, and quotes the entire source as evidence. The validator accepts that different step because formal validity, a verbatim quote, and matching atom names do not establish that the quoted text asserts the inference. This is a semantic false acceptance, not an observed watermark false positive. The accepted step is only a forced completion; it supplies no branching-choice iToken. Passing it through the current structural detector returns `status="insufficient_evidence"`, `n_choices=0`, `p_value=null`, and `detected=null`. See [detector](real_model_pilot.py) and [choice extraction](pipeline.py).

The [six scoring contexts](research_results/qwen_05b_dense_fp32_v1/scores_summary.json) correctly group nine terminated textual candidates into three canonical choices. Saved cached-versus-uncached token scores agree within a maximum absolute difference of approximately `2.70e-5` natural-log units; every candidate was checked in the open context and only the first candidate in each other context. However, the nine candidates cover only `8.43e-20` of the open-context probability mass, and approximately 6.26%–14.74% in the five contexts containing a candidate menu. Thus the normalized groups are probabilities for predicting iTokens **conditional on those listed terminated expressions**. They are not estimates of the full next-iToken distribution under unrestricted generation.

The benchmark is a small, fixed, agent-authored set in a closed symbolic domain with explicit atom names, not independent human data or a representative sample of unrestricted ideas. Development adaptation and the separate frozen ID protocol are appropriate to report, but development performance is not held-out evidence. The observed 1/5 semantic false-acceptance fraction is descriptive, not a population rate. Frozen extractors, runners, and gold labels were not changed by this review.
