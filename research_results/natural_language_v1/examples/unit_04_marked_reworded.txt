New members get a picture guide to the studio and walk through it at their own pace. Ask them to talk the steward through a session afterward so there is time to clear up any gaps before they start working on their own. For each round of bookings, the steward puts up a list showing whose turn it is to choose from the session sheet. Give different people the first picks from round to round. Each member chooses among the remaining bench times to suit their schedule. A person who turns up can use a session nobody has taken.

Try the material on a small working sample before planning the rest of the project. With the results in hand, the member puts the next steps, the supplies to get ready and the intended stopping point on their project card. Tools each have a numbered hook and a matching space for the borrower's name. Sign there before taking the tool, and bring it back during the same session. Someone who comes across an empty hook can see whom to ask. If something feels or looks wrong in use, set the tool aside immediately in the tray for faulty tools. Whoever spotted the fault should describe the experience on a repair card, giving the steward both a tool safely withdrawn from use and particulars for the person inspecting it.

Members can leave a short question under the relevant subject on the help list and get on with any work they can safely manage. The steward can bring people with the same question together for one explanation. A question that crops up repeatedly also makes a good choice for the week's optional demonstration. Invite members to the demonstration corner so those new to the technique have a chance to learn it before they run into difficulty.

Tell the people around the noisy-work station when you are going to hammer and wait for them to respond before you begin. They may need a moment to put delicate work on hold. Anyone leaving a wet piece to dry chooses an empty spot on the rack and labels the piece with their name and the earliest safe handling time. Others can then judge when drying space might become available. To store a project until another visit, pack it, label it with the owner's name and a date for review, and take it to the steward. The steward allocates a vacant shelf space within that member's usual allowance and records where to retrieve the project.

As you produce usable, clean scraps, compare them with the samples beside the labeled bins and drop them into the appropriate one for others to use. For spare material belonging to individual members, the steward collects offers labeled with the amounts and the conditions attached. Bring the owners and prospective takers together once a week so they can discuss an offer before anyone accepts. At the close of each day the studio opens, the steward takes stock of shared supplies and updates the restocking card with the counts and any deliveries already on order. This lets the person ordering consider several shortages at once.

Neighbors should spend the last few minutes of a session checking both their benches together. Finish the cleanup list at one before moving to the other so that both people can look for missed scraps and tools. When members next come in, have them read the dated notices on the board and mark their acknowledgment beside their names before they start. The steward can see who needs an individual update. Schedule a walk around the studio together for the outgoing and incoming stewards during their overlap. Enter unfinished jobs in the handover pad along the way so the incoming steward sees where supplies are running low or repairs are needed and knows what remains to be done.
