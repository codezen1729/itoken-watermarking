Ask a volunteer on each block to visit neighbors who may have household items to offer. From those conversations, report the expected quantities in each category to the organizers. This includes people who might overlook a form and gives organizers an idea of the volume to expect. Every item still needs to pass the advertised checks on arrival. Let contributors sign up for an available intake period so they can plan their trips and volunteers can anticipate busy spells. Leave some capacity for unscheduled arrivals in every period. For staffing, the coordinator takes volunteers’ preferences and available hours into account when assigning jobs. Send the roster out early enough for people to get ready and for uncovered shifts to be filled.

Meet each contributor with two volunteers. One goes over the condition questions with the owner as the other examines the goods, so questions about a missing accessory or a shaky joint can be settled on the spot. For each item that passes, the volunteer who conducted the interview transfers the answers to the usual label, entering the item number, a description, any limitations and the accessories supplied. Visitors then see each owner’s account in a consistent format. Also record whether the contributor wants unsought goods back or is happy to donate them at closing.

Organize the tables by household room and sign them accordingly, making it easy to head straight for the goods one needs. Give every complete set its own tray and a single label naming all its components so visitors can handle the pieces and still tell which belong together. Use reserve shelving for excess stock and leave the aisles open. When goods leave a display, the table volunteer asks for the kinds of items needed and a runner brings suitable replacements that are available in reserve.

The welcome volunteer takes a few new arrivals at a time through browsing, making a claim, holding goods and picking them up. Make clear that anyone can take goods free of charge and that contributions are voluntary. Once the group sets off, visitors who want more help can stay at the entrance diagram with the volunteer. Have a request volunteer take descriptions from people searching for something missing from the display. List the kinds of goods wanted on the board and look for matches among reserves and new arrivals, where an item might turn up before going on display.

For competing claims on an available piece, the volunteer enters each interested person’s name and the time they asked on the sheet. When the brief decision period ends, award the piece to the earliest recorded claimant. Anyone can check the times behind the decision. At the point of claiming, a recipient who needs a hand carrying something can reserve a short appointment. They bring their collection record to meet the helper at the appointed time for the move to the pickup area.

A recipient who wants to keep looking can take their claimed items to the volunteer responsible for holds. Agree on a pickup time no more than twenty minutes away. Before placing the goods on the shelf, the volunteer notes the recipient’s name and the deadline, making it clear who has the goods until pickup and when to return. To collect, first show the claim record to the recording volunteer. That volunteer confirms the claim, logs the departure and checks off a collection slip. Show the slip when taking the goods out so the paperwork is complete by the exit handover.

When browsing ends, use the contributors’ recorded instructions to divide the remaining goods into returns and donations before letting people in for closing collection. Contributors can locate their own goods, and the donations are together for the pickup arranged to follow. Leave an optional brief comment card at the exit for visitors, donors and volunteers. Ask how they got on with finding things, the steps for claiming them and the running of the day. Their answers capture fresh recollections and let organizers see where different groups ran into the same problem.
