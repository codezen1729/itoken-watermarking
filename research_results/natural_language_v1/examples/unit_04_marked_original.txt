Give newcomers the illustrated studio guide and let them take as long as they need to walk the route. At the end, have each newcomer describe to the steward how a session works. The steward can pick up on anything they missed before they work independently. For booking, the steward posts the order in which members may choose sessions from the sheet. Rotate who gets an early choice each round. Members can fit the benches still available to their own schedules, and anyone arriving can take an unclaimed session.

Start a project with a small working sample. Once a member has seen how the material behaves, they can use the project card to set out what to do next, what supplies to lay out and where they expect to stop. Before lifting a tool off its numbered hook, the member writes their name on the corresponding line. Anyone who finds the hook empty can go straight to the borrower, who returns the tool within the session. If a tool proves faulty, stop using it and put it in the marked tray. The person who noticed the problem fills in a repair card with what they saw or felt during use. The steward then has the tool out of circulation and a specific account to pass on for inspection.

Put brief questions on the help list under their topics. The steward can call together members puzzled by the same technique and show them once, while other members carry on with work they can safely do. If one question keeps coming back, the steward can choose it for that week's optional demonstration and invite members over to the demonstration corner. Someone just starting the technique may learn it before reaching the same sticking point.

Before hammering at the noisy-work station, announce what you are about to do and wait for acknowledgment from the people nearby. That gives someone doing delicate work a chance to pause. For a wet piece, its maker picks a vacant rack space and tags the piece there with their name and the earliest time it can safely be handled. The next person looking for drying space can see when a place might open up. For work going into storage between visits, pack it and mark the owner and review date. Show it to the steward, who assigns a free shelf position and logs the location so the work can be found again within the member's usual allowance.

Sort clean, useful offcuts as you make them. Match the piece to the examples by the labeled containers and put it straight into the right one for someone else to use. For members offering their own spare materials, the steward gathers labeled offers showing quantities and conditions. Hold a weekly exchange so owners can meet people interested in taking an offer and answer questions before they accept it. At the end of every opening day, the steward counts common supplies and brings the restock card up to date, including any orders already due. Several low stocks can then be considered together when deciding what to order.

Reserve the last few minutes at neighboring benches for the two members to work as a pair. Go through the cleanup checklist together on one bench, then do the same on the other. A second pair of eyes helps spot a forgotten tool or scraps. Before starting their next session, members read any dated changes on the board and acknowledge them by their names. The steward can follow up individually with anyone who still needs to hear the update. During the shift overlap, both stewards walk around together and write down outstanding duties on the handover pad as they go. The incoming steward has seen the low shelf or maintenance issue behind the note and knows which follow-ups are still owed.
