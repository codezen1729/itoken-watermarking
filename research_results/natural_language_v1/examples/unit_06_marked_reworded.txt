Start with the times the rehearsal room is free. The person handling bookings sends the choir a proposed run of dates and gives singers a chance to flag conflicts while there is still room to move things around. Then book the series. At a consistent interval before each booked rehearsal, send a reminder with a link for singers to say whether they'll be there. Pass a summary to the conductor early enough to plan around the expected turnout in each part.

Tell singers which numbered scores they'll need to bring and circulate the approved digital copies early enough for a read at home. The organizer lays out the matching paper copies for people to collect as they arrive. Ask for volunteers to take turns coming early for setup and remind each person of their particular job, whether it's arranging chairs, laying out folders or getting the conductor's stand ready. Rotating the duties spreads the early starts around.

Before setting the rehearsal order, the conductor asks each section leader which prepared pieces their part would most benefit from singing again. Allow for the different sections' needs and the time booked, then share the order ahead of the session. At the outset, tell the singers which stretch of work will end with the main break. That way the choir knows when it will finish a task and rest together, and anyone needing a brief absence can still step out.

Display the familiar warmup sequence approved by the conductor. A different section leader guides each stage, giving several people a short turn leading the choir through its preparation. With the singers seated, have them sing a short phrase for the conductor to assess the sound in the occupied room. The conductor can then ask singers to move their chairs within their sections, keeping accessible routes clear and making sure the cues will be visible from every new seat.

When the choir struggles with a phrase, the conductor listens to what the singers are doing and picks the single likeliest source of the difficulty. Practice that feature in isolation before singing it in the context of the phrase again. During sectional time the conductor stays with the part needing personal attention. The other leaders work simultaneously in separate corners with their own singers on the bars they've been given. All the parts come back together at the end of the allotted time.

Demonstrate how a common entry or cutoff will be signaled and ask singers to mark the cue in their music so they'll remember to look up. After the parts have sung a passage together, hear from one section at a time about what reached their ears. Take turns asking different sections so the conductor hears from around the room before choosing an adjustment. As the conductor changes a dynamic or the shaping of a phrase, singers pencil it into their scores. Revisit the revised bars aloud before dismissal so singers can catch anything they misunderstood. The organizer then circulates the agreed markings with the piece and bar for each.

Group the materials for home practice by the tricky passage that prompted them. For each passage, the conductor indicates the bars and the recordings available for each part, so someone who remembers struggling in rehearsal can find their own line. Close the session by suggesting two priorities for anyone choosing to practice, and specify the bars in the rehearsal notes. Send absent singers those notes and links to both the revised markings and the recordings. They can update their copies and cover the missed work when it suits them before they return.
