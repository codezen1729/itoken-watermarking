A guest in the drinks queue should be able to arrange a cup and keep their place. Send a volunteer along the queue to ask who would like to borrow one and write up the request cards. The volunteer takes the cards to both the drink counter and the cup station so each sees the same requests. When demand exceeds the number of clean cups on hand, ask people when they actually need to use one. Give priority to the earliest stated need. Someone picking up a drink for a session later in the afternoon can wait for the next supply.

Put the return map on the counter where guests can consult it before choosing a cup. Leave small copies by the cups for anyone who wants to carry the directions away. At lending, the volunteer enters a distinguishing description of the cup in the record and invites the guest to write their own planned return time alongside it. That gives the guest a say in the commitment and the volunteers a way to recognize the cup afterward. Ask at this same station what would make bringing the cup back easier. Pass those comments straight to the return points while there is still a chance to help the people borrowing now. If someone wants to keep a cup longer, they can tell a volunteer how much extra time they need. The volunteer works out the new time with them and enters it in the record.

At a busy return point, assign two people to a short stretch of duty. One stays with the people arriving, accepts each used cup, gives it a quick check and puts it in a washable crate. The other stays with the lending record. As soon as a small crate of returns has accumulated, the recorder identifies the cups by their descriptions and marks the corresponding entries together. Guests can leave immediately, and the recorder can finish a batch without repeatedly breaking off to receive cups.

When a crate is ready, mark it on the transfer list. That mark calls for a volunteer to make the short trip to the washing station, so crates move as returns accumulate. After the usual wash, the person washing enters the clean cups on the available-stock list before carrying them to the lending station. That advance notice lets the lending team prepare for the people it can serve next. During a shortage, have one person bring the stock notice up to date while another goes through the requests with waiting guests. For overdue cups, take a reminder sign around the return points first. After the round, bring the announcement board up to date with the number still outstanding. This reaches guests where they can act on the reminder without keeping their contact details.

Arrange a brief overlap at each shift change. As each task passes from one volunteer to another, the outgoing and incoming teams update the handover note together so it reflects who has taken responsibility. After the final return count, put some spare cups at the lending station and some on the reserve shelf for next time. Size those shares to the expected lengths of the service blocks, leaving enough close at hand for the opening stretch. To choose the next change, start with the comment guests made most often. Compare it with the number of cups lent and the times they came back, and use that check to select a change that addresses a difficulty visible in actual use.
