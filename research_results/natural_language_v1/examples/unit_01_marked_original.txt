A guest should be able to ask for a loan where they order their drink. The counter volunteer sends a request card ahead to the cup station, giving the people there a chance to have a cup ready by the time the guest arrives. Set aside a few available cups for each upcoming period of service and take requests against that period's supply. This gives people arriving later a chance to borrow, even if the first rush would have taken every cup on the shelf.

At the cup station, write down the distinguishing details of the cup and hand the record to the guest to enter the time they expect to return it. That entry is their commitment for the loan. Give them a little return map with the cup and trace the route on the counter map so they can recognize their way back. While they are there, ask what would make it easier for them to return the cup. Pass their comments to the return points while the gathering is under way, giving those volunteers time to help before the guests make the trip.

People who need their cups longer can tell a volunteer how much more time they need. The volunteer works out the new time with them and writes it in the lending record so both know when the cup is due. For cups that go overdue, put a short, clearly timed return period on the announcement board for everyone. After that period has passed, collect the latest figures from the return points. Guests get a common opportunity to bring cups back, and the café can follow up on its stock using the figures from the gathering.

At a busy return point, assign one person to take cups and another to keep the record for a short stint. The first person accepts each cup, looks it over immediately and places it in a collection crate. Once a small crate has accumulated, the second person matches all the descriptions in that crate to their entries in the lending record. Guests can move on as soon as their cups have been received. Mark a crate ready on the transfer list when it is ready to leave. That mark calls for a volunteer to carry it to washing, so the trips follow the flow of actual returns.

After the regular wash, the washing volunteer puts the clean cups on the available-stock list before taking them back to lending. The people handing them out can see what is on its way. If supplies run short, have one volunteer update the stock notice at the same time that another works down the request list with waiting guests. During a shift overlap, the outgoing and incoming volunteers update their note together as each task changes hands, making clear who now has the list, the next crate or the counter.

Count the returned stock when planning for the next gathering. Start with a modest supply at the lending station and keep the rest of the spare cups on the nearby shelf. Bring more forward as the request list lengthens. To decide what else to change, begin with the comment heard most often from guests. Check the lending totals and actual return times for evidence of that difficulty, and choose a trial that addresses the difficulty those records support.
