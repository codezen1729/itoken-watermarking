Greet each guest face to face. The host can ask whether they feel like joining a game now or would prefer a few minutes to get comfortable, then note their name and preference on the welcome sheet. That gives the host a starting point for forming groups and gives anyone who came alone a familiar face. Let guests browse the games and mark the ones that catch their interest. The host can suggest games based on those marks and take requests for anything else in the collection that's available.

Invite people who are happy to teach to announce which games they could start. Interested guests can join them for a closer look, ask what playing would involve and meet the other prospective players. Before they start, the host checks that the game accommodates the group. Guests are free to sit this one out and join a later game. When someone arrives late, a welcome volunteer can introduce them at a table nearing the end of play. If they agree to play the next game together, the volunteer makes a note. While waiting, the guest can try any activity from the waiting basket. They return it when the table calls and take the place already agreed.

Lay the printed rules aid where everyone can consult it while the teacher explains the essentials. Once everyone has heard the explanation, take questions and give each player a trial turn with an unfamiliar action. After the last trial, restore the starting position before anyone makes a choice that counts. Learners who would like tactical advice can choose a willing player as their partner before play begins. They can ask that person again whenever they need help. The partner talks over the options quietly, using only what the players involved can see, and leaves the actual move to the learner.

Work back from closing to leave enough time to put the game away. Each group agrees on a finish time, displays it on a card and asks one of the players to warn the table as it draws near. Agree in advance how to record a position if the group wants to resume an unfinished game. The players also choose points during play for a visible timer to give a signal. They can check the time left at those points without anyone having to watch the clock throughout. When players disagree over a rule, stop play and let two of them spend a short time looking it up while the rest wait. Hear their conclusion and use their interpretation. If the question is still open, use the pair's provisional decision for the evening and check it afterward.

Choose a player at each table to keep an ear on the volume. If a long chat is getting loud, they can invite the people talking to continue in the conversation corner before it gets hard to hear play at the next table. A player wanting a snack or drink can arrange a short time away with the group, use the refreshment area and return for their next action. A player who needs a personal break tells the table aloud. Find a place to stop right away, say what action is due next, keep private information protected and hold off on any move that needs the absent player.

At the end of play, everyone helps sort the components into their labeled bags. Before people leave the table, the teacher checks each bag against the list so the whole group is still on hand to look for anything missing. Put the verified game back in the collection. The following day the host invites brief, optional feedback on the games available, how people found a game to join and anything that got in the way of taking part. Guests have had time to reflect by then. Grouping the answers before arranging another evening helps the host recognize issues shared across tables.
