Start with the room’s available times. The organizer draws up a tentative rehearsal calendar from those openings and shows it to the singers so they can identify specific clashes. Revise the dates in light of the responses before making the bookings. For each confirmed rehearsal, ask singers to enter their expected attendance on the shared sheet by the day before. The conductor can plan for the mix of voices expected.

Circulate the pieces in a numbered list with copies of the scores in digital form so members can work on them at home. Set out matching paper copies for them to collect on arrival. Before settling the rehearsal plan, have the conductor consult the section leaders about difficulties that remain after preparation. The conductor can then publish a balanced sequence that includes time for the choir to rehearse together on the troublesome passages.

The setup coordinator should be there ahead of the singers with a list of tasks. As helpers appear, assign them to arrange seating, lay out folders, put up the stand and cover the rest of the list according to who is there. At the outset, the conductor announces a short span of time in which the main break will fall. That allows a stop at a musically convenient point and still lets singers step away separately as needed.

Display the sequence of approved exercises for the usual warmup. Assign successive portions to different section leaders so everyone knows whose direction to follow. Then listen to a brief phrase from the choir to judge the acoustics. The conductor can reposition chairs within each voice group in response, making sure every singer can see the beat and there is still a clear way through.

Demonstrate the cue for a shared entrance or cutoff before singing it. Have the singers mark the spot in their scores to remind them to watch for the gesture. If a passage is giving trouble, let the conductor hear it through and identify its main difficulty. Isolate that aspect, such as words, notes or rhythm, then sing the whole passage again. When the conductor wants to work with one section, send the others to different corners, each with its own leader and music assigned for the purpose. Reassemble within the allotted time to hear the results in the full choir.

After everyone has sung together, ask sections in turn what balance they heard. Calling on different parts in rotation gives the conductor perspectives from across the room when deciding on adjustments. Have singers pencil changes into their scores as they are given. Before dismissal, the conductor reads aloud through the bars in question so discrepancies can be corrected. The organizer circulates the agreed markings, identifying the piece and bar for each.

Close by identifying two things singers can choose to practice at home. Include the bars in the rehearsal notes so they can go straight to them. After each rehearsal the section leaders send their respective singers a short sequence of approved recordings, arranged in practice order and pointing to the bars requiring attention. Send absentees the notes along with links to both the changes and the recordings, giving them time to amend their scores and listen before they return.
