Let readers choose among the afternoons and collection times they can manage, ranking those options on their request sheets. Staff should consider the sheets together before allocating shelf space. Two readers with the same first choice may both be happy with appointments that bring them in at different times. Record each booking under the reader’s name with the title requested. Their message that they are setting out is the cue to prepare the title from that list. This lets someone book well ahead without drawing a helper away from people collecting now.

Allocate each book a place on whichever shelf has space. Write the spot on the desk list next to the name and appointment time, so either piece of information will lead staff to it. For a missed collection, offer the reader a choice of times still available. Once they choose, move the book and book the additional period that was being held for them.

People taking a seat should find the quiet-waiting notice there when the doors open. A helper can welcome several arrivals, record their names and carry the sheet to the desk at intervals. At the greeting, ask each person whether they would like help finding or carrying anything and record the help they choose. Use the wait to deal with everyday questions. A helper can ask what people need to know and write the answers on the question pad, so readers are ready when called.

Work the collection desk in short periods. As each begins, check the waits for people with appointments and those without, then serve the group that has waited longer first. In this way a lengthening walk-in queue gets attention too. Send a written account of who is present to the helpers preparing books. They can fill the delivery tray with titles for those people. If service runs late, staff should speak to the affected readers while a helper changes the public notice. On the way out, a helper can hand readers a comment card and show them where to drop it, freeing the desk for collections.

Set aside a place for people to leave returns with a slip they fill out themselves. They can go once they have left their books. Helpers use the slips to inspect the books in the order received. As soon as a few have been checked, put them on the trolley in shelf order so a single quick trip puts them back in use.

An outgoing helper should make a list of unfinished work before the relief helper arrives. While both are there, go over the list together. To plan the following afternoon, use the closing review to identify the worst delays in the log. Read the comments about those particular stages and choose one change based on readers’ experiences there.
