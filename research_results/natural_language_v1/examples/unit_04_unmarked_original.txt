At the beginning of a new member’s first visit, ask an experienced member to walk through the illustrated guide with them. The two can follow the route a project takes through the studio, with the experienced member showing how the studio works at each stop. Give the newcomer that same person’s name as someone to ask for help as the first project takes shape.

For each booking round the steward posts the order in which members may choose bench times. Rotate who goes first from round to round. Each person picks a session that suits them from the openings left, and anyone arriving can use an unclaimed session. Before going to the supply shelves, a member fills in a project card with the next task, what it will take and how far they intend to get today. Looking at the card again when they finish gives them a place to pick up next time.

Write your name against a tool’s hook number before lifting it down. Someone looking for a missing tool can go straight to its borrower, who returns it within the booked session. If it develops a fault, stop using it, put it in the marked tray and fill out a repair card with what you were doing and what you noticed. The steward can pass those observations along when getting the tool inspected. For a question about the work itself, enter it under its topic on the help list. The steward can bring people facing the same difficulty together for a short explanation while others carry on working.

Post regular, short windows for hammering and other noisy work. Members can pick an open turn at the designated station and fit quieter work around the posted times. For wet work, the maker chooses a vacant spot on the drying rack and tags the piece with their name and the earliest time it can safely be moved. Someone who will soon need rack space can see when a spot might open up.

At the end of a task, match clean, useful offcuts to the samples on the scrap shelf and put them straight into the corresponding containers. The next person can use them as soon as they’re sorted. For unfinished work, pack the piece and label it with the owner and review date, then take it to the steward. The steward chooses an open shelf position within the member’s allowance and logs the location for retrieval. Neighbors at the benches should spend their last few minutes together, doing the cleanup checklist at one bench and then the other. A second pair of eyes helps catch a tool or scrap tucked behind a project.

Each day at closing, the steward counts the common materials and brings the restock card up to date, including what is already on order. Looking over all the shortages together helps the steward decide how to spend the reorder allowance. Members with surplus materials of their own can leave the steward a labeled offer giving the amount and terms. Gather owners and prospective recipients at a weekly exchange so they can ask about the material and agree on a handover in person.

For the weekly optional demonstration, let members post techniques they would like to see and vote on the suggestions. The steward then arranges the selected lesson in the demonstration corner. When a studio routine changes, date the notice and have the steward point it out and explain it when each member next arrives. Members can ask how it affects the work they are about to do. During the overlap between stewards, have the two walk through the room together and enter outstanding duties in the handover pad as they go. The person taking over will have seen the low shelf or damaged tool behind the entry.
