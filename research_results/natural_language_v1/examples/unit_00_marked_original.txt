Give readers space on the request sheet to rank the windows when they could come in. With those rankings in hand, staff can spread pickups over the afternoon and reserve shelf space for each window. The preparation list should show who is coming for each title and when. Staff use that list to get a book ready shortly before its reader is due, leaving room for the books needed in the meantime. Give every requested book a labeled shelf location and enter it on the desk copy alongside the reader and window. A staff member can look up a reader and go straight to the indicated spot regardless of how the neighboring books are arranged.

Have a helper run the tray between preparation and pickup on a fixed round. The helper checks the transfer list when loading the tray and again on delivery, so neither station has to call for individual titles. If someone misses a window, staff contact them with the windows that still have space. Leave the book where it is until the reader chooses a new time, then move it for the extra service period.

At the entrance, display the quiet-waiting notice with a small map showing where people can sit away from the desk. An arriving reader can choose a spot on the way in. A helper takes names there and brings the arrival sheet to the desk at regular intervals, giving staff several names at once. The helper also offers to find or carry books and lists the requests readers accept. Someone who turns out to need a hand that day can ask on the spot without giving a reason.

Divide desk time into short blocks and alternate between appointments and walk-ins. Even on an afternoon when every appointment is booked, walk-ins will know their turn is coming. Finish handing over the books first. If a reader still has a routine question, write it on the pad so a helper can follow up while the desk handles the next collection. Give the reader a feedback card with their books and invite them to drop it in the box by the exit, where there is time to write. When a pickup is delayed, have a helper keep the posted notice current while desk staff talk directly with the readers concerned.

Set aside a place for returns where readers can leave each book beside its completed slip. Helpers use the arrival information on the slips to check the books in order, freeing readers to leave once they have set theirs down. On the trolley, group checked books by their shelf destinations. A helper can then take all the books for one stretch of shelving in a single trip.

Before shifts overlap, the outgoing helper should write down the work still pending. Use the overlap to talk the incoming helper through that record. At closing, put forward one practical change for tomorrow. Check what the wait log says about the problem and what readers wrote on their cards. Agree to try the change once the team can explain how it fits both.
