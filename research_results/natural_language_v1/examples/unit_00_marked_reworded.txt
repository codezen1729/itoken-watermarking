Ask readers filling out a request to list their possible pickup times in order of preference. Staff can use the answers to distribute visits across the afternoon and allocate shelf space by time slot. On the preparation list, include the reader's name and arrival time for each book. Preparing titles just ahead of those times keeps the shelves available for earlier pickups. Assign each book a marked spot and copy that location into the desk register with the reader's name and time. Anyone at the desk can find a book by name without knowing the order of the books around it.

Send a helper around with the transfer tray at set intervals. Checking the tray against the list both when it leaves preparation and when it reaches pickup saves both stations from having to call about each book. If a reader misses a pickup, offer a choice of times that still have room. Keep the book in its original spot until the reader picks a time, then move it for that additional pickup period.

Post a small seating map beside the quiet-waiting sign at the entrance so readers can decide where to sit away from the counter as they arrive. A helper visits the seats to collect names and takes the sheet up to the desk periodically so staff can act on several arrivals together. Have the helper offer to locate books or carry them and note which offers people take up. A reader can request help then and there, whatever the reason.

Switch the desk between booked pickups and walk-ins every few minutes so even a full appointment schedule leaves a predictable turn for people who drop in. Complete each handover before dealing with routine questions. Jot down any that remain for a helper to answer while the next reader collects their books. Hand out comment cards with the books and point out the box at the exit so people can take their time writing. During a delay, put a helper in charge of updating the notice as the desk staff speak with the people whose collections are affected.

At the returns area, ask readers to leave the filled-in slip next to the book. The slip shows when it came in, so helpers can process returns in arrival order after the readers leave. Load the checked books onto the trolley according to where they belong, letting a helper reshelve the books for one section together.

The helper finishing a shift should list unfinished jobs before the next helper arrives and go over them together during the overlap. Close the day by proposing one change the team could make tomorrow. Look at both the recorded waits and the readers' feedback on the difficulty. Agree on a trial once the team can say how the proposed change answers what both records show.
