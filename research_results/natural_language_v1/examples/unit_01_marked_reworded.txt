Let people request a borrowed cup while placing their drink order. The counter volunteer can send the card straight to the lending station so volunteers there can prepare for the person's arrival. Earmark part of the cup supply for each service period and fill requests from the cups allotted to that period. That leaves cups for later guests even on a day with a large early crowd.

When issuing a cup, the volunteer notes the features that distinguish it from the others. Ask the guest to write their promised return time on that same record. Show them the way back on the counter map and give them a small map to take along. Also ask what would help with the return. Get these suggestions to the volunteers at the collection points during the gathering so they can act before the guests arrive.

A guest who needs an extension can explain how much longer they want the cup. The volunteer and guest settle on a revised due time and the volunteer records their agreement. If cups are overdue, announce a brief return window with clear times on the board. Once everyone has had that chance to bring a cup in, get up-to-date counts from the return points for the café's stock follow-up.

Where returns are busy, split the work between a receiver and a record keeper for a short stretch. The receiver takes and inspects each cup on the spot and places it in a crate. Guests can leave immediately. When a small crate's worth has come in, the record keeper checks the descriptions of its contents against the loan entries as a batch. A tick on the transfer list when a crate is ready tells a volunteer to take it for washing, so collections happen as the cups come in.

After the usual washing, the volunteer lists the clean cups as available and then delivers them to the lending station. This gives that station advance notice of the incoming supply. When stock is tight, one person keeps the stock notice current while a second goes through the request list with the guests who are waiting. At shift change, the volunteers coming and going revise the handover note together as they pass over each duty, recording who has taken charge of the counter, the list or the next crate.

Use a count of the cups returned to plan the next gathering. Put a small quantity out for lending at the start, leaving the remaining reserve on a shelf close by to replenish the station as requests build up. For other improvements, take the point guests raised most frequently and compare it with the lending counts and the times when cups actually came back. Try a change directed at the problem borne out by those records.
