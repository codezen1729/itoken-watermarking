Give a first-time studio member a tour as soon as they arrive. Pair them with an experienced member and have them use the pictures in the guide to trace a project’s journey around the room. At each stop the guide can show what happens there. Make sure the newcomer knows their guide’s name so they can turn to the same person as they work on their first piece.

At booking time the steward puts up a list showing whose turn it is to choose, with a different member getting first choice each round. Members go down the list, each choosing a convenient bench session from the times still free. Someone who turns up on the day can take any session nobody has claimed. Before collecting materials, fill out a card describing the next job, the supplies it needs and the stopping point you have in mind for today. Consult it again before leaving so you know where to resume.

The hook numbers double as a way of finding tools in use. Sign your name next to the number before taking the tool, and have it back before your session ends. Anyone needing it can see whom to ask. If it seems faulty, stop work with it and leave it in the designated tray. Describe the job and the symptoms on a repair card for the steward to share with whoever examines it. If you need help with a technique, write your question under the relevant heading on the help list. That lets the steward give a brief explanation to several members who need it without interrupting the rest of the room.

Schedule brief, recurring periods for loud jobs and display the times. Members can take a free turn at the station for noisy work and plan their quieter jobs around it. On the drying rack, choose an empty place yourself and mark your piece with your name and the first time it can be moved safely. Other makers can then estimate when there will be space for their own pieces.

When you finish a job, compare any clean, reusable scraps with the examples on the offcut shelf. Drop each into its matching bin so the next maker can find it immediately. Wrap up anything still in progress, add the owner’s name and the date for review, and hand it to the steward. The steward finds a vacant spot within that member’s storage allowance and records where the package went. Leave a few minutes to clean up with the person at the next bench. Run through the checklist together at each bench in turn so one of you can spot things the other might miss behind the work.

At closing every day, the steward counts shared supplies and updates the restocking card with quantities still needed and orders already placed. Seeing the shortages side by side helps with allocating the reorder budget. A member offering extra material can give the steward a labeled offer stating how much is available and on what terms. At the weekly exchange, prospective recipients can meet the owners, ask questions and arrange the transfer face to face.

Members propose and vote on techniques for the optional weekly demonstration. The steward organizes the chosen topic in the demonstration corner. Put a date on any notice changing a studio practice. The steward draws each member’s attention to it on their next visit and talks it through so they can ask about their own upcoming work. When stewards change shifts, use their time together for a circuit of the studio. Enter unfinished duties in the handover pad along the way so the incoming steward can connect an entry to the shelf that needs filling or the tool awaiting attention.
