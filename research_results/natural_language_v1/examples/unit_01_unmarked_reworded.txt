Take requests to borrow cups from guests as they stand in line for drinks. A volunteer can fill in the cards as they go and take them to the drinks counter and the cup station, keeping both teams informed while guests hold their places. If there are fewer clean cups than requests, find out when each guest expects to use the cup. Serve the soonest need first. Someone who only needs theirs for a later session can get one from the next lot.

Display the map of return points on the counter so guests can check it before picking a cup. Keep pocket copies beside the cups too. The volunteer lending a cup should note what makes it recognizable and ask the borrower to enter the time they intend to return it. Letting guests set that time gives them a part in the agreement. Ask there and then what would help them return it, and relay the answers immediately to those staffing the return points so they can act during this round of loans. A borrower who needs an extension can explain how much longer they need, agree on a revised time with a volunteer and have the volunteer record it.

For a short spell at a busy drop-off, have two volunteers work alongside each other. The first takes cups from guests, looks them over and sets them in a washable crate, at which point guests are free to go. The second looks after the loan register. Once a small crate has filled, that volunteer can match the cups to their descriptions and check them off in one sitting while the first keeps receiving returns.

An entry on the transfer list signals that a crate is ready for a volunteer to carry to the nearby wash station. This keeps the crates moving at the rate they fill. After washing the cups as usual, the washer should add them to the clean stock tally before taking them over to lending. The lending volunteers can then get ready for the next guests they have cups for. When supplies run low, split the work between someone updating the stock notice and someone reviewing requests with the people waiting. If loans have fallen overdue, carry a reminder sign on a circuit of the drop-off points. Then post the outstanding count on the announcement board. Guests see the reminder somewhere they can return a cup, and volunteers have no need to retain contact information.

Allow a few minutes for the two shifts to work together. As each duty changes hands, both teams should amend the handover note to show who now has it. Once the last returns have been counted, divide the spares between the lending station and the reserve shelf. Base the split on how long the coming periods of service should last and keep the opening period’s supply within reach. For the next improvement, take the most frequent guest comment and check it against lending totals and actual return times. Choose a change that tackles a problem borne out by those records.
