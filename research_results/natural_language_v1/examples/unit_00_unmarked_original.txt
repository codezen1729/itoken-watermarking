Give readers a chance to say which afternoons and times work for them. On the request sheet, ask them to put the available windows in order of preference. Before reserving any shelf space, staff can look across those rankings and find acceptable times for readers who might otherwise all come in the same rush. A reservation goes on the preparation list with the reader’s name and title. When the reader confirms they are on their way, staff use that entry to get the book ready. Someone who expects to collect later can have a window without tying up a helper in the middle of the rush.

Give each requested book an assigned shelf spot. On the list at the desk, keep that location alongside the reader’s name and window. Staff can find a title from either detail while using whatever shelf space is available. If a reader misses their time, contact them with the windows that still have room. Wait for their choice before moving the book and arranging the extra service period on hold.

Before opening, put the quiet-waiting notice beside the places where people can sit, so they see it as they settle in. Have a helper greet arrivals, take their names and bring the arrival sheet to the desk at regular intervals. Several people can check in during one trip to the desk. At that first meeting, offer help locating or carrying books and list the requests people accept. A reader can decide on the spot what help would make the visit easier. While people are waiting, a helper can invite routine questions and jot the answers on the question pad. That leaves people ready to collect when their turn comes.

At the start of each short block at the desk, compare how long booked readers and walk-ins have been waiting and start with the group experiencing the greater wait. This gives people who come in off the street a turn as delays build. The desk sends the preparation area a written list of people actually waiting. Helpers there load the transfer tray with the books on that list so each delivery serves readers already present. When a delay occurs, have a helper update the public notice while staff explain it personally to the readers concerned. At the exit, a helper can offer a feedback card and point out the box so the collection desk stays available for the next person.

Give returns a separate place. Readers fill in a slip and leave it beside their book. Helpers use the slips to take returns in arrival order for their condition check, with no need to hold readers for an intake interview. Make up small loads of checked books on the trolley and arrange each load by shelf location. A helper can get a few titles back into circulation promptly and shelve them in one short circuit.

Before the next helper arrives for a shift change, the departing helper writes down the jobs still open. Use the overlap to talk the incoming helper through that list. At closing, find the longest waits in the day’s log and read what readers said about those parts of their visit. Use their accounts to pick one adjustment for the next afternoon at a point where the log shows people lost time.
