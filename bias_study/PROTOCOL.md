# Stronger selection among free reasoning steps

The experimental unit is one fresh public problem with an independently drawn target key. Two author agents each provide four everyday problems and 24 distinct compatible short arguments per problem before generating keys. The problem topics were specified when commissioning the authors. Each candidate can be expressed in ordinary language. Separate readers extract any number of open premise, action and consequence triples, each with a verbatim passage supporting it. The experiment admits triples under the previous study’s citation and deduplication rules. Candidates with zero admitted triples are unavailable in every arm. We require at least six eligible candidates per problem. Raw first responses remain available.

## How a key changes which arguments get chosen

The encoder and local Yes/No usefulness question are identical to the previous open study. For each candidate, Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct supplies the log probability of the single token Yes conditional on the two tokens Yes and No. We add one independent Gumbel draw per candidate, shared across the three arms. The base utility of a set is the average of those six noisy log weights. Zero watermark strength gives the usual top-six selection by these perturbed log weights. The authors' probabilities for all possible next arguments are unavailable. The resulting policy operates on a sample of free-written candidates.

We use three independently domain-separated SHA-256 subkeys of the problem's target key. Each subkey produces a signed direction per triple field under the earlier problem-dependent SHAKE256 algorithm. A step’s coordinate in each direction is the average projected value of the three fields. The set reward is minus the average of the three population variances, with every accepted reasoning triple in the subset contributing one observation. A keyed set of arguments earns a higher score when its projections cluster tightly. Shifting or reordering the complete set of coordinates leaves its variance unchanged.

For all eligible sets of six snippets (134,596 sets with 24 eligible snippets), standardize reward to mean zero and population SD one within the generator pool. Constant reward is inert. Maximize base utility plus strength times standardized reward. Strength is 0 for controls, 0.2 for the weak comparison and 12 for the strong arm. Break ties with the lexicographically first set. Publish passages in source order, grouped in paragraphs of four then two. Because the Gumbels are per snippet, this implements a randomized selection policy over content without assigning native LLM probabilities to six-argument sets. Model usefulness and overlap with control are descriptive checks on how much this changes selection.

Previous open-study material served as development data. A methods agent simulated different choice rules and found that within-document dispersion can be influenced at planning time but sensitive to independent readings. We chose an exact subset search and a weak arm so that the effect of changing the bias can be tested on the same fresh corpus with an identical detector.

## How detection and preservation are tested

Draw one 256-bit target and 199 independent surrogate keys once per problem. The selector uses only the target. Each original gets one first-pass free rewrite. Writers are asked to preserve the entire reasoning and can reorganize sentences or substitute vocabulary. Original and rewrite extraction agents receive the public problem and one passage only. They have no target step count, generator pool, scores, keys, previous reading or answer catalog. Packets contain one version per problem in a mixture of arms. Agents work in fresh contexts with access restricted by instruction. Two further independent readers see each original–rewrite pair, without scores or extractions. A rewrite counts as faithful if both judge all its substantive reasoning preserved. Changed or uncertain judgments, or disagreement, exclude a pair only from the conditional estimate. Judges run even on initially undetectable texts.

The catalog-free detector requires a public problem, a document reading, and the verifier’s keys. It scores all supported steps with exactly the same raw negative mean variance as the generator. Fewer than two admitted steps gives an abstention. The one-sided Monte Carlo p-value is (1 + number of the 199 surrogate statistics at least as large as the target statistic)/200. Flag a passage at p ≤ 0.01. No training files, pool normalization, desired center, or list of possible steps is needed for detection. We do not resample keys or outputs on the basis of detection, pick the best rewrite, or change thresholds. Fix the protocol, source readings and numerical code by hashing them before drawing keys.

Report three distinct quantities for each arm: all positive plans, originals and rewrites out of all eight problems; positives among all rewrites both judges consider faithful; and positives among faithful rewrites of initially positive originals. A zero conditional denominator is undefined. Also report joint success over eight, changes in original detections for the strong vs weak and control arms, votes, step counts, edit extent and selection overlap. Each problem contributes once per arm. A positive on one short curated case cannot establish a general survival probability. These eight problems were written for this experiment and the readers are models.
