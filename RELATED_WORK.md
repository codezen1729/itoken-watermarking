# Where this proposal sits

We want to watermark the choices that make up an idea, so that changing its wording does not automatically remove the mark. There is already substantial work on watermarking semantic units, planning choices, and reasoning paths. We therefore need to make a narrower claim: define a useful formal unit of reasoning, check its validity, specify exactly which changes leave it unchanged, and test a watermark over the resulting choices.

The following six papers were checked against their primary research pages. Dates distinguish the first preprint from a conference publication where that publication was verified. An arXiv entry establishes availability of a preprint; it does not by itself establish peer review or validate its results.

| Paper | Verified timing | What overlaps with our proposal | What we still need to distinguish |
|---|---|---|---|
| [SemStamp: A Semantic Watermark with Paraphrastic Robustness for Text Generation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.226/), Hou et al. | [First preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03991): 6 October 2023. NAACL: June 2024. | Hashes sentence embeddings into semantic regions and uses rejection sampling to favor watermarked regions. It already moves the watermark beyond individual text tokens. | Its units are regions of an embedding space. Our proposed units are explicit inference applications with premises, a rule, and a conclusion. Formal validity and a stated equivalence relation would need to add something useful. |
| [A Semantic Invariant Robust Watermark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06356), Liu et al. | First preprint: 10 October 2023. ICLR 2024; latest listed revision: 19 May 2024. | Uses the semantics of the preceding text to determine watermark logits, making the signal less dependent on exact preceding words. | The watermark still acts on token logits. Our proposal changes the sampling of a complete logical transition. Semantic context alone is therefore not our novelty. |
| [AgentMark: Utility-Preserving Behavioral Watermarking for Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03294), Huang et al. | First preprint: 5 January 2026; revised 24 April 2026. | Elicits a distribution over planning behaviors and embeds identifiers through distribution-preserving conditional sampling. This is already watermarking probabilistic choices above the token level. | It relies on an elicited behavior distribution and logged planning decisions. Its discussion identifies extracting planning policies directly from logits or latent representations as future work. We need to distinguish a measured distribution over formal reasoning steps from a model's self-reported probabilities. |
| [On Protecting Agentic Systems' Intellectual Property via Watermarking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08401), Wang et al. | First preprint: 9 February 2026. | AGENTWM biases the distribution over functionally equivalent tool-execution paths. This closely matches the idea of several paths leading to the same resolution. | Its carrier is an observable tool trajectory. Our intended carrier is a formally checked reasoning structure reconstructed from the delivered explanation. Merely replacing a tool action with the name “iToken” would not establish a new contribution. |
| [R-CoT: A Reasoning-Layer Watermark via Redundant Chain-of-Thought in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25247), Zhang et al. | First preprint: 28 April 2026. | Trains a distinctive reasoning path that preserves the final answer. The broad idea of watermarking reasoning paths has therefore already been proposed. | R-CoT uses a trigger-activated redundant self-verification policy learned through GRPO, and verifies model ownership through triggered queries. Our prototype instead samples among ordinary valid proof alternatives and detects a statistical mark in the resulting artifact. |
| [Semantic Behavioral Watermarking: Paraphrase-Robust and Forgery-Resistant Provenance for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08668), Ji et al. | First preprint: 6 October 2026. | SBW maps actions to semantic clusters, conditions on recent cluster history, and uses keyed assignments to favor selected choices. It is the closest algorithmic comparison to the proposed iToken mechanism. | SBW uses learned embedding clusters over actions. We need to show what explicit inference rules and proof dependencies buy us. Its paper also leaves important replay and capacity limitations open; those problems do not disappear just because we use formal units. |

The earlier citation `arXiv:2610.08668` was verified directly. Both its [abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08668) and [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08668v1) identify it as Semantic Behavioral Watermarking, submitted on 6 October 2026.

## What we can reasonably investigate

Our potential contribution is a watermark over **checked inference applications and their dependencies**, together with a precise account of which rewrites preserve those units. This is narrower than watermarking meaning or behavior in general. We should compare it against semantic clustering and ordinary text-token watermarking, rather than claim that all previous work only operates on words.

Another useful question is whether we can estimate an iToken's probability by aggregating actual model probabilities across its textual realizations. The probability sum itself is standard mathematics. A contribution would be an estimator with measured coverage and calibration, and evidence that its errors do not undermine generation or detection. The finite reference predictor in this prototype does not establish that result for a trained LLM.

We have not established that this is the first formal-inference watermark. This focused search found no exact match to the proposed contract, but a novelty claim needs a broader review and a concrete comparison. We also should not claim that a valid watermark proves who first conceived an idea. At most, a calibrated detector provides evidence that an artifact passed through the keyed generation procedure, subject to its attack model.

## Two further comparisons to keep in view

[Watermarking LLM Agent Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18700), Meng et al., first submitted 21 February 2026, introduces ActHook: triggered hook actions in training trajectories for detecting downstream reuse. [Echoes within the Reasoning: Stealthy and Effective Watermarking via Chain of Thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28890), Lu et al., submitted 27 May 2026, introduces BiCoT: model-ownership signals in the representation geometry of reasoning anchors. Both matter when distinguishing artifact provenance from trained-model or dataset ownership.
